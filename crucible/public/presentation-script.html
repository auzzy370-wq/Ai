01

Title

~45 sec

“Thanks for the time. I’m going to walk you through CRUCIBLE — an elite software engineering and cybersecurity qualification platform. The short version: candidates don’t solve puzzles. They design, build, secure, test, deploy, and recover real systems. Résumé claims are never treated as proof.”

Do: Pause on the three stats — 19 challenges, 3 tracks, ≥90 elite bar. Let that land before advancing.

02

The hiring gap

~1 min

“Everyone in this room already knows the pain. We hire ‘senior’ talent off résumés and whiteboards, then discover in month three that they can’t ship, secure, or own a system under pressure. Claims aren’t competence. Algorithm drills aren’t production ownership. And security is often a separate process — or skipped entirely. Crucible replaces that theater with a controlled proving ground: isolated workspaces, hidden tests, deploy checks, incident pressure, and a human final gate.”

Do: Gesture left “what breaks,” right “what replaces it.” Don’t read every bullet — pick two that match your audience.

03

What it is / isn’t

~1 min

“Important positioning: this is not LeetCode, not trivia, and not a take-home form you hope they didn’t ChatGPT. It is a proving ground — full product and security challenges, a real browser IDE, sandboxed execution, evidence-based AI scoring, plus architecture, incidents, and team delivery. And the operating principle is non-negotiable: AI assists. Humans decide.”

Do: Hit the callout line slowly. If someone asks about AI bias, say scoring is evidence-bound and a reviewer still signs off.

04

Three tracks

~1 min

“There are three tracks. Software Engineering covers SaaS, realtime, distributed systems, databases — five challenges today. Cybersecurity is first-class, not an add-on — ten challenges spanning AppSec, cloud hardening, SOC and IR, cryptography, zero trust, detection, IAM, and more. Secure Full-Stack is product delivery with an elevated security bar — ecommerce, AI SaaS, incidents, team builds. Nineteen published challenges total. Same platform, different pressure profiles.”

Do: If the room is security-heavy, linger on the cyber row. If product/eng, emphasize secure full-stack as the bridge.

05

Who goes through Crucible

~45 sec

“The product maps to how hiring actually works. Hiring managers create assessments and assign candidates. Candidates build. Team members collaborate on shared delivery. Technical reviewers approve or reject with documented reasoning. Super admins own platform and AI configuration. So this isn’t only a candidate tool — it’s an operations surface for elite qualification.”

Do: One pass down the table. Don’t over-explain RBAC unless asked.

06

End-to-end flow

~1.5 min

“Here’s the loop, start to finish. One — pick a challenge from the library. Two — publish an assessment with a scoring policy. Three — assign the candidate; we provision an isolated workspace with starters and tests already placed. Four — they build in the IDE: commit, run public tests, hidden tests, deploy verification. Five — they submit; AI evaluates from evidence, not invented results. Six — they still have to prove more: architecture package, incident or SOC simulation, follow-ups, optional team challenge. Seven — a human reviewer decides. Eight — score report and skill lanes update. That’s the whole product story in eight steps.”

Do: Walk 1→8 with your finger or cursor. Emphasize steps 6 and 7 — this is where you differentiate from “CI turned green.”

07

Platform surfaces

~1 min

“If you’re wondering what lives in the product today: operations dashboards, challenge library, assessments, a full browser IDE, architecture submissions, incident sims, team challenges, and a technical review queue — plus admin for AI providers and security configuration. It’s mobile-friendly too, but the power demo is still the IDE and the review flow.”

Do: Don’t read every card. Name 4–5: Library, Assessments, IDE, Incidents, Review.

08

Elite scoring bar

~1.5 min

“Scoring is a hundred-point weighted model across ten categories — frontend through collaboration. For software engineering, backend and architecture carry the most weight. For cybersecurity, security jumps to twenty-five and problem-solving to fifteen. The track changes what ‘elite’ means. Qualification is intentionally hard: overall ninety or better, eighty percent in critical categories, successful deploy and hidden tests, incident recovery, architecture, team when required, no critical vulnerabilities — and a human must approve. We calibrate to challenge difficulty. We do not sell population percentiles.”

Do: Read the yellow callout almost verbatim — it’s your credibility slide. Pause after “human approval.”

09

Four skill evidence lanes

~1 min

“This is the hiring philosophy in one diagram. Claimed skills — what they say. Portfolio — what they link. Assessment-verified — what the assessment measured. Human-reviewed — what a reviewer signed. Most platforms collapse those into one green checkmark. We don’t. A résumé claim never graduates to ‘verified’ without evidence.”

Do: Contrast weak signals (top) vs measured/signed (bottom). Good Q&A hook.

10

Trust & execution

~1 min

“For the technical audience: candidate code, uploads, and AI prompts are untrusted by default. Execution can run in a constrained process mode for demos, or Docker sandboxes with CPU, memory, and timeout limits. Production guidance is stronger isolation — microVMs or gVisor — not ‘mount the Docker socket and hope.’ Stack is Next.js, TypeScript, Prisma on Postgres, Monaco editor, optional Redis worker.”

Do: Keep this short unless you’re in a security/architecture room — then offer to go deeper offline.

11

Demo path

~30 sec setup + ~8 min live

“If we have time, here’s the live path — about eight minutes. Hiring manager assigns from the library. Candidate opens the workspace, runs tests, submits. We glance the cybersecurity challenge library. We show incidents or architecture — proof it’s not just green CI. Reviewer makes the human call. Seed accounts are ready when demo env passwords are configured locally — never share those values in slides or chat.”

Do: If skipping live demo, say: “I’ll show this offline — the seeded environment already has assignments and workspaces with tests placed.” If demoing, switch windows now; keep this slide visible as a checklist.

12

Close

~45 sec

“Closing line: résumés claim. Crucible verifies. We’ve built an evidence pipeline for elite hiring — real builds, real security pressure, AI-assisted scoring, and a human final gate — so qualification means demonstrated ability. Happy to take questions — or jump straight into the product.”

Do: Stop talking. Hold the slide. First question usually comes on scoring, AI, or isolation — you’re ready from slides 8–10.

Optional Q&A anchors
“How do you stop AI hallucination on scores?” → Evidence-bound evaluation; no invented test results; human gate.
“Is Docker enough?” → Demo mode yes; hostile prod workloads need stronger isolation (called out in docs).
“Why not just hackathons?” → Repeatable scoring policy, hidden tests, audit trail, reviewer accountability.
